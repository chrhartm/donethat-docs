AI surfaces how peer benchmarks and competitor pricing compare to the selected supplier's quote, flagging negotiation leverage points.

## What it is, how it works

Pressure analysis shows how a selected supplier's quote compares with peer benchmarks and competitor pricing, then points at the specific lines where your position is strongest. It answers a narrower question than benchmarking: not what the market charges, but where this quote is weak.

- Comparison runs line by line, since the advantage usually sits in a few items rather than the total.
- Peer benchmarks come from your own multi-supplier history in the same category.
- Output names the lines worth challenging and the evidence supporting each challenge.

## When not to use it

- Line-level comparison needs comparable line items. Where suppliers bundle differently, the comparison misleads more than it helps.
- Pressing every flagged line damages relationships for marginal gain. Pick the two or three that matter.
- In categories with genuinely few suppliers, there's no competitive pressure to analyze and the model will manufacture the appearance of some.

## How to get started

- Apply it to categories where you hold several suppliers and have real comparative data.
- Ask for the three highest-value gaps rather than a full line-by-line report. Buyers act on a short list.
- Record which challenges succeeded, so the model learns what your suppliers actually concede.

## Best practices / further reading

- Feed the findings into [negotiation position generation](./negotiation-position-generation.md) so those points land in the brief.
- Persistent gaps across a category are a signal for [category opportunity identification](./category-opportunity-identification.md).
- Benchmarking data appears in [Sievo](https://sievo.com/).
