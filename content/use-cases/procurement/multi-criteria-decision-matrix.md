AI weights price, quality, risk, ESG, and strategic fit to produce a ranked supplier recommendation with a traceable rationale.

## What it is, how it works

The matrix combines price, quality, risk, ESG, and strategic fit into one ranked recommendation, with the weighting made explicit and the reasoning for each score traceable back to evidence. It replaces the spreadsheet that usually carries this decision.

- Weights are agreed before bids are opened, which is what stops them being tuned to favor a preferred outcome.
- Each dimension carries its evidence, so a challenged score can be defended line by line.
- The output shows how close the ranking is, not just who won.

## When not to use it

- Weighted scoring creates an appearance of objectivity that the underlying judgments may not support. Where a decision is genuinely strategic, say so rather than dressing it as arithmetic.
- Assigning weights after seeing the bids invalidates the exercise, and reviewers will spot it.
- Two suppliers separated by a fraction of a point aren't meaningfully different. Treat near-ties as ties and decide on other grounds.

## How to get started

- Agree the weights in writing with the business stakeholder before the RFP is issued.
- Run the matrix alongside the panel's existing process for one cycle rather than replacing it.
- Publish the sensitivity: if a small weight change flips the ranking, the decision needs more evidence, not more math.

## Best practices / further reading

- The cost dimension should come from [total cost of ownership modeling](./total-cost-of-ownership-modeling.md) rather than headline bid price.
- Risk inputs come from [ESG supplier screening](./esg-supplier-screening.md) and [single source risk flagging](./single-source-risk-flagging.md).
- Sourcing suites with weighted scoring include [GEP](https://www.gep.com/) and [Zycus](https://www.zycus.com/).
