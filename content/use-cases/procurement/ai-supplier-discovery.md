ML scans external databases and web sources to identify new suppliers beyond the approved list, scored for category fit, geography, and ESG, using tools like Tealbook or SAP Ariba.

## What it is, how it works

Discovery widens the shortlist past the suppliers you already know. The model searches external registries, company data, and the open web for firms that fit a category, then scores each on capability, geography, certification, and ESG posture.

- Search is driven by your category specification, so results are ranked against stated requirements rather than generic relevance.
- Enrichment pulls in financials, certifications, and location before a buyer sees the list.
- The output is a longlist for human qualification, not a list of approved vendors.

## When not to use it

- Discovery is worthless if you can't onboard the suppliers it finds. Check your qualification backlog before adding to the funnel.
- Regulated categories with mandated supplier panels have no room for new entrants, whatever the model surfaces.
- Data on small and non-Western suppliers is patchy, and a low score often reflects thin coverage rather than a weak supplier.

## How to get started

- Run it against a category you've already sourced and see whether it finds the incumbent. That's a cheap sanity check on coverage.
- Ask for a longlist of twenty and have a category manager mark which are genuinely credible. The hit rate tells you if it's worth continuing.
- Agree what qualification steps still apply. Discovery shortens the search, it doesn't replace due diligence.

## Best practices / further reading

- Screen the longlist with [ESG supplier screening](./esg-supplier-screening.md) before inviting anyone to bid.
- Suppliers that survive qualification should carry into [market price benchmarking](./market-price-benchmarking.md) so the RFQ starts from a defensible number.
- Supplier discovery platforms include [Zycus](https://www.zycus.com/) and [GEP](https://www.gep.com/).
