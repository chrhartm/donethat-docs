AI surfaces contracts approaching expiry, summarizes supplier performance, and drafts a renewal recommendation brief, using tools like Ironclad or Zip.

## What it is, how it works

Renewal alerting surfaces contracts approaching expiry with enough notice to act, summarizes how the supplier performed, and drafts a recommendation. It exists because auto-renewal deadlines pass quietly, and your negotiating position goes with them.

- Lead time is set by category, since a complex re-sourcing needs months and a commodity renewal needs weeks.
- The brief combines performance data, spend, and current market position.
- Auto-renewal and notice-period clauses are highlighted specifically, because those are the dates that actually bind.

## When not to use it

- Alerting depends on accurate contract metadata. If renewal dates were never captured properly, extraction has to come first.
- Alerts sent too late are worse than none, since they signal a deadline you can no longer influence.
- A recommendation drafted from performance data alone misses relationship context the category manager holds.

## How to get started

- Audit renewal dates and notice periods across your top contracts by value. Missing dates are the real problem, not the alerting.
- Set lead times per category with the people who run those sourcing events.
- Route alerts to a named owner rather than a shared mailbox.
