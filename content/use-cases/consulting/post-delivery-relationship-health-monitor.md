LLM monitors communication patterns after engagement close and flags declining contact frequency or key-stakeholder turnover.

## What it is, how it works

Relationship monitoring watches contact patterns after an engagement closes and flags decline: contact frequency falling away, your sponsor leaving, the account going quiet. Lapsed relationships are cheaper to revive than new ones are to build.

- Signals are contact frequency and stakeholder changes rather than sentiment.
- Sponsor departure is treated as a distinct high-priority event.
- Alerts suggest a specific person to contact and a reason to do it.

## When not to use it

- Monitoring client contact after an engagement ends can feel like surveillance if it becomes visible. Keep it to data you legitimately hold.
- Contact decline after close is normal, and alerting on it generates busywork.
- An alert with no genuine reason to reach out produces exactly the empty check-in email clients dislike.

## How to get started

- Monitor accounts you actually want to return to, not the whole client list.
- Treat sponsor departure as the primary trigger. It is the clearest signal and the most actionable.
- Require a real reason for contact before anyone reaches out.
