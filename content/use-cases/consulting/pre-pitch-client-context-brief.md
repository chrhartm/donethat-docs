RAG aggregates client news, filings, and prior engagement history into a structured intelligence brief for the pitch team.

## What it is, how it works

A context brief pulls together what the pitch team should know: recent filings, news, leadership changes, and your own history with the account. Retrieval does the assembly, so the team argues about strategy instead of hunting for facts.

- Public sources and prior engagement records are combined into one document.
- The brief leads with what changed since last contact, which is the only genuinely new part.
- Prior work with the client is surfaced even when it sat in another practice.

## When not to use it

- A brief full of company boilerplate teaches people to skip it. If it says nothing they did not know, it failed.
- Private and mid-market clients have thin public data. The brief will pad rather than admit the gap.
- Reciting a retrieved fact you do not understand is worse than not knowing it.

## How to get started

- Run it for one pitch team for a month and ask which sections they actually used.
- Put prior engagement history first. That is the part no analyst can find quickly.
- Deliver it into the calendar invite. A brief in another tool does not get read.
