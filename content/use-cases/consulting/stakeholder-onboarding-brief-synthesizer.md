RAG over client filings, news, and prior meeting notes produces role-specific onboarding briefs for each incoming consultant.

## What it is, how it works

Onboarding briefs give each incoming consultant what their role needs to know about the client, drawn from filings, news, and prior meeting notes. An analyst joining in week three otherwise learns the context by asking people who are busy.

- Briefs are role-specific, so an analyst and a partner get different documents.
- Prior engagement history is included, including work from other practices.
- The brief updates as the engagement progresses rather than being written once.

## When not to use it

- Client information has confidentiality boundaries, and generating a brief that spreads sensitive detail across a wide team is a real risk.
- Generic company background gets skipped. Role relevance is the whole value.
- A brief cannot replace the conversation with the workstream lead, and treating it as sufficient onboarding leaves people underprepared.

## How to get started

- Start with the analyst role, where the context gap is largest and the material least sensitive.
- Include prior engagement history first. That is what new joiners cannot find themselves.
- Ask joiners after two weeks what they still had to ask about, and add that.
