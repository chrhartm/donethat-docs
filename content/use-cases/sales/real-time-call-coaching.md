In-call prompts surface missed questions, objections, and battle cards as the conversation happens, using tools like Cresta or Balto.

## What it is, how it works

Live coaching listens during the call and surfaces prompts as the conversation happens: a battle card when a competitor is named, a suggested question when a pain goes unexplored, a rebuttal when a familiar objection lands.

- Prompts are triggered by what is said, within a few seconds, or they arrive too late to use.
- Content comes from your own approved material rather than generated on the spot.
- Everything is visible only to the rep.

## When not to use it

- Cognitive load is the real constraint. A rep reading prompts is not listening to the buyer, and the call gets worse.
- Latency has to be very low. Prompts that arrive after the moment has passed actively distract.
- Experienced reps generally find it intrusive. The value concentrates in onboarding and in unfamiliar territory.

## How to get started

- Deploy to new hires first, where the benefit is clearest and the disruption most acceptable.
- Limit to two or three trigger types. More prompts means less listening.
- Ask reps to turn it off when it hurts, and pay attention to when they do.

## Best practices / further reading

- Post-call review through [methodology scorecard](./methodology-scorecard.md) is less intrusive and often more useful.
- The rehearsal alternative is [negotiation role-play simulator](./negotiation-role-play-simulator.md), which carries none of the live risk.
- Live coaching tools include [Cresta](https://www.cresta.com/) and [Balto](https://balto.ai/).
