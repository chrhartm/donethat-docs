AI synthesizes discovery, proposal, and commitments into an onboarding document for the CS team, using tools like Gong or Read.ai.

## What it is, how it works

Handoff briefing turns everything the sales team learned into a document the customer success team can act on: what the customer is trying to achieve, what was promised, who matters on their side, and what nearly killed the deal.

- The brief is assembled from discovery calls, the proposal, and the contract rather than from a rep's memory at handoff.
- Commitments are listed explicitly, since those are what a customer will hold you to.
- Known risks carry over instead of being quietly dropped at the boundary.

## When not to use it

- A generated brief nobody reads is the same failure as the handoff call nobody attends. The document is not the mechanism, the conversation is.
- Summaries that omit the awkward parts, such as a promise made under pressure, are worse than no brief because CS trusts them.
- Where sales and CS use disconnected systems, the brief becomes another artifact to maintain by hand.

## How to get started

- Include a mandatory commitments section and have the AE confirm it. That single field prevents most early churn surprises.
- Keep the brief to one page. Length is what stops it being read.
- Ask CS which sections they used after thirty days and cut the rest.
