An LLM scores calls against MEDDPICC, SPIN, or Sandler frameworks, using tools like Hyperbound.

## What it is, how it works

Scorecarding rates a call against whichever framework your team runs, MEDDPICC, SPIN, or Sandler, and shows which elements were covered and which were not. It makes coaching consistent across managers who otherwise each have their own idea of a good call.

- Scoring criteria come from the framework you actually train on, not a generic rubric.
- Each score points to the passage supporting it, so a rep can dispute it with evidence.
- Trends over time matter more than any single call score.

## When not to use it

- Frameworks are heuristics, not truth. A rep who closes consistently while scoring badly is telling you something about the framework.
- Tying scores to compensation guarantees gaming, and the calls will start being performed for the scorer.
- If managers do not coach on the output, the score is just surveillance with extra steps.

## How to get started

- Calibrate against calls your best managers have already scored by hand. Disagreement means the rubric is wrong, not the manager.
- Use it on trends per rep over a quarter rather than call by call.
- Publish the rubric to the team. Hidden scoring criteria destroy trust immediately.
