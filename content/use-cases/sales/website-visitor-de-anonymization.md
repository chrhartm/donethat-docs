An identity graph reveals which target accounts browse pricing and product pages, using tools like RB2B or Warmly.

## What it is, how it works

Visitor identification matches anonymous web traffic against an identity graph to tell you which companies, and sometimes which people, looked at your pricing page. It converts passive traffic into a list someone can act on.

- Company-level matching is the reliable part and works from IP and network data.
- Person-level identification depends on the vendor's data sources and is far less certain.
- Page-level detail matters more than the visit itself, since pricing and comparison pages carry intent.

## When not to use it

- Person-level de-anonymization has real privacy exposure, and rules differ sharply by jurisdiction. This needs a legal opinion before deployment, not after.
- Acting too directly on the data unsettles buyers. A rep referencing a specific page visit reads as surveillance.
- Match rates on small companies and remote workforces are low, and vendors rarely lead with that.

## How to get started

- Decide the privacy position first, including which regions you'll run it in and what reps may reference.
- Start at company level with high-intent pages only. That's most of the value at a fraction of the risk.
- Route matches into account research rather than into immediate outreach.

## Best practices / further reading

- Matched accounts are worth checking against [ICP lookalike account discovery](./icp-lookalike-account-discovery.md) before a rep spends time.
- Visits from existing accounts belong in retention monitoring, not prospecting.
- Intent signals pair with triggers from [buying signal trigger monitoring](./buying-signal-trigger-monitoring.md).
- Identification vendors include [RB2B](https://rb2b.com/) and [Warmly](https://www.warmly.ai/).
