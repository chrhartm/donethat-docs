NLP and graph analysis identify decision-makers, champions, and blockers across the account, using tools like People.ai.

## What it is, how it works

Committee mapping builds the org picture around a deal: who decides, who influences, who blocks, and who has gone quiet. It reads meeting attendance, email threads, and org data to show the account as a network rather than a contact list.

- Relationships are inferred from actual interaction rather than from titles.
- Coverage gaps are the point. The map shows who you have not met.
- Engagement decay per contact is visible, which is often the earliest sign of trouble.

## When not to use it

- Inferred influence is a guess. Titles mislead, and so does email volume, since the busiest correspondent is frequently not the decision maker.
- In deals where you only ever talk to one person, the map has nothing to work with and will imply a committee that you have not actually seen.
- Communication data carries privacy obligations that vary by region, particularly where personal accounts are involved.

## How to get started

- Compare the generated map with the rep's own understanding on three live deals.
- Focus on coverage gaps at the economic-buyer level, which is where deals most often stall.
- Refresh before every major stage gate rather than once at qualification.
